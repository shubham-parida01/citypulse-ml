# CityPulse --- Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Version:** 2.0\
**Status:** Draft / Implementation Baseline\
**Product:** CityPulse\
**Product Type:** AI-Assisted Urban Incident Reporting, Triage &
Resolution Platform\
**Primary Users:** Citizens, Municipal Operators, Field Officers, System
Administrators\
**Primary Stack:** React, Spring Boot, FastAPI/Python, PostgreSQL +
PostGIS\
**Routing Model:** Manual, human-controlled departmental routing\
**AI Role:** Image classification, confidence estimation, severity
assessment, and decision support

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Executive Summary

CityPulse is a citizen-first urban incident reporting and municipal
workflow platform designed to reduce the friction of reporting civic
problems and reduce the manual effort required to understand and triage
incoming reports.

Citizens can report urban incidents such as potholes, waterlogging,
garbage accumulation, broken streetlights, sewage overflow, illegal
dumping, damaged sidewalks, fallen trees, and other infrastructure
problems by submitting a photograph and geographic location.

After a report is submitted, the CityPulse backend sends the image to an
AI computer-vision microservice. The AI analyzes the image and returns
structured information including:

-   probable incident category
-   model confidence
-   estimated severity
-   detected visual/contextual indicators
-   model version

The AI does **not** automatically decide which municipal department
should receive the incident.

Instead, the AI acts as a decision-support layer. A municipal operator
reviews the incident, its image, location, AI analysis, and severity
recommendation, and then manually routes the incident to the appropriate
department, division, and field team.

The complete lifecycle is:

**Citizen Report → AI Analysis → Human Review → Manual Routing → Field
Assignment → Work → Resolution → Citizen Verification → Closure**

This human-in-the-loop model is a deliberate product decision. It keeps
organizational and operational decisions accountable to municipal
personnel while using AI where it provides the most value: extracting
structured information and helping prioritize incoming incidents.

# 2. Problem Statement

Citizens frequently encounter localized urban problems that affect
safety, mobility, cleanliness, accessibility, and quality of life.

Examples include:

-   severe potholes
-   damaged roads
-   broken streetlights
-   waterlogging
-   sewage overflow
-   garbage accumulation
-   illegal dumping
-   damaged sidewalks
-   fallen trees
-   damaged public infrastructure

A traditional complaint workflow can create friction at two levels.

## 2.1 Citizen-side problems

Citizens may have to:

-   identify the correct complaint category
-   determine the responsible department
-   provide detailed descriptions
-   navigate complicated forms
-   repeatedly check complaint status
-   communicate with authorities when a problem is incorrectly
    categorized

## 2.2 Municipal-side problems

Municipal teams may receive a large number of reports with:

-   inconsistent descriptions
-   incomplete information
-   varying image quality
-   incorrect categories
-   unclear severity
-   duplicate reports
-   insufficient location context

Operators must manually inspect and organize these reports before they
can be routed and acted upon.

## 2.3 CityPulse opportunity

CityPulse addresses this by inserting an AI-assisted triage layer
between citizen submission and municipal review.

The system should automatically answer:

> "What does this report appear to be, and how urgent does it appear?"

The municipal operator remains responsible for:

> "Who should handle it and how should it be acted upon?"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. Product Vision

> Make reporting an urban problem as easy as taking a photograph while
> giving municipal teams structured intelligence, transparent workflows,
> and actionable information to resolve incidents efficiently.

The long-term vision is to evolve CityPulse from a reactive
complaint-management platform into an urban incident intelligence
platform capable of identifying spatial and temporal patterns in civic
problems.

# 4. Product Principles

## 4.1 Citizen-first

Reporting should require as little effort as possible.

Target interaction:

**Open → Photograph → Location → Submit**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.2 Human-in-the-loop

AI should assist municipal workers rather than replace operational
decision-making.

AI provides:

-   classification
-   confidence
-   severity
-   supporting evidence

Humans provide:

-   final classification when needed
-   department routing
-   assignment
-   operational judgment
-   resolution approval

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.3 AI must fail safely

If the AI service is unavailable or uncertain, the incident must still
exist.

An AI failure must never result in:

-   lost report
-   inaccessible incident
-   automatic rejection
-   incorrect assignment

The incident should fall back to manual review.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.4 Explainability over magic

The system should avoid presenting unexplained AI outputs.

Instead of only:

> Severity = 8.7

the system should expose supporting factors where possible:

-   large visible damage
-   standing water
-   roadway obstruction
-   apparent traffic hazard

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.5 Accountability

Important workflow changes must be recorded.

Every assignment, correction, priority override, status change, and
resolution should be traceable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 4.6 Privacy by default

Users should only see information required for their role.

A field officer needs incident information and location, but does not
necessarily need the citizen's private contact details.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. Goals

## G1 --- Reduce reporting friction

Allow a citizen to create an incident with minimal interaction.

## G2 --- Automate incident understanding

Use computer vision to identify likely incident categories.

## G3 --- Automate initial triage

Provide a severity estimate and recommended urgency.

## G4 --- Reduce operator workload

Present structured AI analysis instead of requiring operators to
interpret every report from scratch.

## G5 --- Preserve human control

Keep routing and assignment decisions manual.

## G6 --- Provide end-to-end tracking

Allow incidents to be tracked from submission through closure.

## G7 --- Improve accountability

Maintain a complete incident history.

## G8 --- Create useful municipal analytics

Allow officials to understand incident patterns geographically and
temporally.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. Non-Goals

The MVP will not:

-   automatically assign departments
-   automatically dispatch field teams
-   replace municipal operators
-   automatically close incidents
-   make legal or enforcement decisions
-   control traffic infrastructure
-   provide emergency response services
-   predict every infrastructure failure
-   integrate with every external municipal system
-   automatically merge duplicate reports
-   perform autonomous field verification

These can be considered future capabilities.

# 9. Core User Journeys

# 9.1 Citizen Reporting Journey

1.  Citizen opens CityPulse.
2.  Citizen selects "Report an Issue."
3.  Camera/upload interface opens.
4.  Citizen captures or selects an image.
5.  Browser requests location permission.
6.  Location is captured.
7.  Citizen optionally adds a description.
8.  Citizen submits.
9.  Backend validates the request.
10. Incident record is created.
11. AI analysis begins.
12. Citizen receives an incident ID.
13. AI analysis completes.
14. Incident becomes available to municipal operators.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9.2 Operator Review Journey

1.  Operator logs in.
2.  Dashboard displays incident queue.
3.  Operator filters/sorts incidents.
4.  Operator opens an incident.
5.  Operator sees:
    -   image
    -   location
    -   description
    -   category
    -   AI confidence
    -   severity
    -   incident history
6.  Operator accepts or corrects classification.
7.  Operator reviews priority.
8.  Operator selects department.
9.  Operator selects division.
10. Operator selects field team.
11. Operator assigns the incident.
12. Assignment is recorded.
13. Field officer is notified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9.3 Field Resolution Journey

1.  Field officer logs in.
2.  Assigned incidents are displayed.
3.  Officer opens incident.
4.  Officer views image and location.
5.  Officer navigates to the incident.
6.  Officer starts work.
7.  Officer performs required maintenance.
8.  Officer uploads resolution evidence.
9.  Officer marks incident as resolved.
10. Citizen receives notification.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9.4 Citizen Verification Journey

1.  Citizen receives resolution notification.
2.  Citizen views incident.
3.  Citizen reviews resolution evidence.
4.  Citizen confirms issue is resolved.
5.  Incident becomes closed.

If the issue remains unresolved:

1.  Citizen rejects resolution.
2.  Incident becomes reopened.
3.  Incident returns to the operational workflow.

# 10. Functional Requirements

# FR-001 --- Registration

The system shall allow citizens to create accounts.

### Required fields

-   name
-   email or mobile number
-   password

### Requirements

-   unique identifier
-   input validation
-   password hashing
-   duplicate-account prevention

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-002 --- Authentication

The system shall authenticate users before accessing protected
functionality.

The system shall support role-based authentication for:

-   CITIZEN
-   OPERATOR
-   FIELD_OFFICER
-   ADMIN

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-003 --- Citizen Profile

Citizens shall be able to view and update permitted profile information.

The system should not expose private profile information to unrelated
users.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-004 --- Create Incident

A citizen shall be able to submit an incident.

### Required

-   image
-   location

### Optional

-   description
-   category
-   additional images

The backend shall generate a unique incident identifier.

Example:

`CP-2026-001042`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-005 --- Image Upload

The system shall accept images from:

-   camera
-   mobile gallery
-   desktop file selection

### Validation

The backend shall validate:

-   MIME type
-   file extension
-   file size
-   image integrity

The system should reject unsupported files.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-006 --- Image Storage

Uploaded images shall be stored separately from the core relational
data.

The database should store:

-   object/storage reference
-   file metadata
-   upload timestamp
-   incident association

The actual binary image should preferably reside in object storage or an
equivalent file-storage layer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-007 --- Geolocation

The frontend shall request browser/device geolocation permission.

The system shall store:

-   latitude
-   longitude
-   accuracy
-   timestamp

The location shall be represented using PostGIS.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-008 --- Location Validation

The backend shall validate geographic input.

Invalid coordinates should be rejected.

The system should preserve reported accuracy so operators can understand
whether a location is precise or approximate.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-009 --- AI Classification

The system shall send the incident image to the AI service.

The AI service shall return a predicted category.

Initial categories:

-   POTHOLE
-   WATERLOGGING
-   GARBAGE
-   ILLEGAL_DUMPING
-   BROKEN_STREETLIGHT
-   SEWAGE_OVERFLOW
-   DAMAGED_SIDEWALK
-   FALLEN_TREE
-   OTHER
-   UNKNOWN

The category set should be configurable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-010 --- AI Confidence

The AI service shall return a confidence score.

Example:

`Pothole — 96%`

The system shall retain the original confidence value.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-011 --- AI Severity

The AI pipeline shall produce a severity score from 0 to 10.

The result shall be stored separately from the operator's final
priority.

This distinction is important:

**AI Severity ≠ Final Operational Priority**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-012 --- Severity Explanation

Where supported, the system shall show factors contributing to the
severity estimate.

Example:

``` text
Severity: 8.7 / 10

Factors:
- Large surface damage
- Standing water
- Roadway obstruction
- Potential traffic hazard
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-013 --- AI Uncertainty

If the model confidence falls below a configurable threshold, the system
shall flag the incident for manual review.

Example:

``` text
Confidence: 42%

AI Result:
Uncertain

Action:
Manual Classification Required
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-014 --- AI Failure

If the AI service cannot process the image:

-   incident remains stored
-   AI status becomes FAILED
-   operator is informed
-   incident enters manual review

No citizen report should be lost.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-015 --- AI Model Versioning

Every AI result shall include the model version.

Example:

`citypulse-yolo-v1`

This allows historical results to be interpreted correctly when models
change.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-016 --- Operator Dashboard

The operator dashboard shall display:

-   total incidents
-   critical incidents
-   high-priority incidents
-   unassigned incidents
-   in-progress incidents
-   overdue incidents
-   recently resolved incidents

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-017 --- Incident Queue

The incident queue shall support:

-   severity sorting
-   date sorting
-   category filtering
-   status filtering
-   assignment filtering
-   department filtering
-   geographic filtering

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-018 --- Incident Details

The operator shall be able to inspect:

-   incident ID
-   image
-   additional images
-   location
-   map
-   citizen description
-   AI category
-   AI confidence
-   AI severity
-   final priority
-   current status
-   assignment
-   status history
-   resolution evidence

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-019 --- Classification Override

Operators shall be able to correct AI classification.

Example:

``` text
AI:
POTHOLE — 62%

Operator:
WATERLOGGING
```

The system shall store:

-   original prediction
-   corrected category
-   operator ID
-   correction timestamp
-   optional reason

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-020 --- Priority Override

The operator may change operational priority.

Example:

``` text
AI Severity: 6.4
Final Priority: CRITICAL

Reason:
Location currently presents an immediate public safety concern.
```

All overrides shall be audited.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-021 --- Manual Department Routing

Operators shall manually select:

1.  Department
2.  Division
3.  Team

The system shall not automatically route incidents in the MVP.

Example:

``` text
Department:
Road Maintenance

Division:
South Zone

Team:
Road Team B
```

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-022 --- Assignment

After selecting the destination, the operator shall assign the incident.

The system shall record:

-   department
-   division
-   team
-   assigning operator
-   assignment time
-   notes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-023 --- Assignment Validation

The backend shall validate that:

-   department exists
-   division belongs to department
-   team belongs to division
-   operator has assignment permission

Invalid combinations shall be rejected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-024 --- Field Officer Dashboard

Field officers shall see only relevant assignments.

Each assignment shall display:

-   incident ID
-   category
-   priority
-   image
-   description
-   location
-   assignment timestamp
-   current status

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-025 --- Start Work

A field officer shall be able to transition an assigned incident to:

`IN_PROGRESS`

The system shall record the timestamp and officer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-026 --- Resolution Evidence

A field officer shall be able to upload:

-   resolution photograph
-   description
-   completion timestamp

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-027 --- Resolve Incident

The field officer shall mark the incident:

`RESOLVED`

The system shall preserve evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-028 --- Citizen Notification

Citizens shall receive an update when:

-   incident is assigned
-   incident enters progress
-   incident is resolved
-   incident is reopened
-   incident is closed

The MVP may use in-app/email notifications.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-029 --- Citizen Verification

Citizens shall be able to confirm or reject a resolution.

Confirmation:

`RESOLVED → CLOSED`

Rejection:

`RESOLVED → REOPENED`

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-030 --- Reopen Incident

A citizen may reopen a recently resolved incident if the problem remains
unresolved.

The system shall record:

-   citizen ID
-   reason
-   timestamp

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-031 --- Incident History

Every incident shall expose a chronological history.

Example:

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-032 --- Audit Logging

The system shall record security-sensitive and workflow-sensitive

Examples:

-   login
-   assignment
-   category override
-   priority override
-   status transition
-   administrative configuration changes

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-033 --- Map Dashboard

The operator dashboard shall display incident locations geographically.

Operators should be able to:

-   zoom
-   pan
-   select incidents
-   filter map markers
-   inspect clusters

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-034 --- GIS Filtering

The system shall support geographic filtering using PostGIS.

Potential queries:

-   incidents within a ward
-   incidents within a radius
-   incidents near a selected point
-   incidents by zone
-   incident density

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-035 --- Analytics

The dashboard shall provide:

-   incident count
-   category distribution
-   severity distribution
-   open/closed ratio
-   average resolution time
-   reopened rate
-   incidents by geographic area
-   incidents over time

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-036 --- Search

Operators shall be able to search incidents by:

-   incident ID
-   category
-   status
-   location
-   department
-   citizen identifier where permitted

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-037 --- Duplicate Detection --- Future

Future versions may identify possible duplicate incidents using:

-   location proximity
-   time proximity
-   category
-   image embeddings/similarity

The system should initially only flag possible duplicates rather than
automatically merge them.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# FR-038 --- Notifications

``` text
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓
```

``` text
    ↓
```

``` text

    ↓

    ↓
```

``` text
    ↓
```

# 18. Non-Functional Requirements

# NFR-001 --- Performance

Normal backend API operations should target sub-500 ms response time
under expected prototype load, excluding long-running AI inference.

AI inference should target approximately 5--10 seconds under the
intended deployment environment.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# NFR-002 --- Availability

The MVP should remain usable even when AI processing fails.

Core incident creation should not depend on successful AI inference.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# NFR-003 --- Scalability

The architecture should allow the following components to scale
independently:

# 30. Key Product Decision: Manual Routing

Manual routing is an intentional architectural boundary.

The AI answers:

> **What is this?**

and:

> **How severe does it appear?**

The municipal operator answers:

> **Who should handle it?**

This separation provides:

-   better accountability
-   simpler implementation
-   easier debugging
-   lower risk of incorrect automated assignments
-   better alignment with real organizational workflows
-   clear human oversight

Future versions can introduce routing recommendations without changing
the fundamental architecture.
